# Writing on the preview

A click in a paragraph, list item or heading writes there, with no mode to
enter: leaving it, or ⌘↵, saves the words that changed to the Markdown, and Esc
puts it back. Citations, values, math, code, note calls and images are locked
chips. Enter breaks a paragraph or opens a new one (in a list, a new item; in a
heading, it goes on to the text), Backspace at its start joins the one before,
emptying one takes it out of the file, the arrows at its edges go to the next
one, and a paste is plain text whose paragraph breaks make paragraphs. ⌘B and
⌘I, or B and I in the selection bar, toggle bold and italic. `@` lists the
paper's references, figures, tables and values, and inserts one as a chip. What
is typed reads literally, so `*` stays an asterisk. Every save is checked before
it is written: built alone, the paragraph must read what you left on the page.
If not, nothing is written, your text stays and the paragraph is flagged until
you edit its Markdown in place or discard it; if the file changed there, you
choose yours, the file's, or the Markdown. New headings, sections, tables,
figures, equations and footnotes stay in the Source tab or the agent's hands.

Inside a paragraph, ⌘Z undoes your typing. Outside the text, it undoes your
latest change in the document, a comment or a saved paragraph.
